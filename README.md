# Korea chip flows 2026: Samsung, SK hynix and foreign selling

Data through Nov 6, 2026. Written Nov 10, 2026.

This note is research for discussion. It is not investment advice.

"Flows" here means net buying and selling of shares, by type of investor, as recorded by the Korea Exchange.

## Summary

Foreign investors' net selling of Korean stocks in 2026 was concentrated in two companies.

- Three Korean newspapers citing Korea Exchange data put Samsung Electronics and SK hynix at 86% to 87% of all foreign net selling of KOSPI stocks from Jan 2 to mid October 2026. One paper printed "about 86%" [S3]. The 87.1% and 87.0% figures are calculated here from the other two papers' amounts [S1, S2].
- By early November the share was about 84% to 86%. The range depends on whether one paper's stock figures end on Nov 4 or Nov 5 (Appendix A2).
- The two companies bought back about 70 trillion won of their own shares in 2026 through Nov 4. That is about 38% of the 183.48 trillion won that foreigners net sold in the two stocks over roughly the same period. This compares sizes only (Appendix A6).
- No buying by either company was found from Apr 21 to Aug 19. That period included May and June, the two heaviest months of foreign selling this year.
- SK hynix's second buyback has up to about 18 trillion won or 13.7 million shares, whichever limit comes first, left in its plan through Jan 28, 2027 (Appendix A6). A plan allows buying but does not require it. Samsung's next step depends on its late January 2027 board meeting.
- Exchange data records what foreigners sold, and gives no reasons.

How far to rely on this: flow and ownership figures come from newspapers, nearly all citing the Korea Exchange. Appendix B marks the exceptions. Buyback figures were checked against the companies' DART filings on Nov 10, 2026, and match. The Korea Exchange's own tables were not pulled, because they need a sign-in with a Korean identity check. Appendix C lists what is still open.

## Terms used

- KOSPI: the main board of the Korea Exchange, Korea's main stock market. KOSDAQ is its second board, for smaller companies.
- Korea Exchange (KRX): the stock exchange operator. It publishes daily net buying and selling by investor type: foreigners, individuals, institutions and companies.
- Net selling: the value of shares an investor type sold, minus the value it bought, over a period. Because it is a net figure, net selling in two stocks can exceed total net selling if the same investors were net buyers of other stocks.
- Two-stock share: foreign net selling of Samsung and SK hynix together, divided by all foreign net selling of KOSPI stocks.
- Percentage points: the difference between two percentages. Moving from 85% to 87% is 2 percentage points.
- Foreigners: investors registered as foreign with the exchange. Two of the papers cited say their figures leave out a small "other foreign" category (기타 외국인) of foreign investors not registered in the usual way [S2, S4].
- Common and preferred shares: Samsung has two listed share classes. Preferred shares usually carry a higher dividend and no vote. The two-stock figures here use Samsung common shares only.
- ETF: exchange traded fund, a basket of stocks that trades like one share. The KOSPI totals here leave ETFs out.
- Buyback: a company buying its own shares on the market. Shares a company holds are treasury shares. Cancelling them removes them from the share count. Shares bought for employee compensation are handed to staff later instead of being cancelled.
- Other corporations (기타법인): the exchange's investor type for non-financial companies trading shares. Company buybacks are recorded here.
- DART: the electronic disclosure system run by Korea's Financial Supervisory Service, where listed companies file reports. OpenDART is its service for downloading those filings in bulk. KIND is the Korea Exchange's own disclosure channel.
- Foreign ownership ratio: the share of a company's shares held by foreigners.
- Trillion won: Korean reports use 조 (jo). 1조 is 1 trillion won. 1억 is 0.0001 trillion won.
- Basis: what a count includes. Two figures on the same basis were counted the same way.

Notes on the table:

- The 86% figure that prompted this note matches the Oct 13 count [S3]. The Hankyung article of Oct 30 that the original alert cited does not contain it [S14].
- Jaekyung printed "about 87%". It appears to have divided by its rounded "more than 210 trillion won" (183.48 / 210 = 87.4%). Jaekyung dates its stock figures only as "according to the Korea Exchange on the 5th". If they run through Nov 5 rather than Nov 4, the share is 84.5% through Nov 5 and 84.4% through Nov 6 (Appendix A2).
- Kukmin's first-half total (149.0464) matches the sum of Herald's January to June figures (149.0463), reading Herald's misprinted February figure "21조731원" as 21조731억 [S1, S2]. ekn's monthly figures also match Herald's to rounding [S7]. So these rows were counted the same way.
- Digital Times reports 203.6976 "from Jan 2 to today", dated Oct 14 [S4]. Seoul Economic Daily reports 203.6977 through Oct 13 [S3]. The near match suggests both are the Oct 13 figure.
- Samsung preferred shares had 5.77 trillion won of foreign net selling by early November [S6]. They are in the totals but not in the two-stock column. Counting them would add about 2.7 percentage points.
- Foreigners net bought 9.66 trillion won of Korean listed ETFs from the start of the year to Oct 12 [S2]. The totals above leave that out.

## Context

- The two stocks' weight in KOSPI market value rose from 48.81% to 50.85% during September [S15]. Foreigners owned about 46% to 55% of the two companies this year [S5, S16], more than their share of KOSPI as a whole, which a Hana Securities analyst put at a record 38.5% in May [S17]. The dates of these figures differ. So the two stocks were probably more than half of what foreigners held in KOSPI. The fair benchmark for the 86% is their weight in foreign-held KOSPI value. Asia Business Daily put it at as much as 63.8% in May, without a date or named source for the figure [S33]. A current figure was not pulled (Appendix C).
- About two thirds of the year's foreign selling came in the first half: 149.05 trillion won, or 67.8% of the Jan 2 to Nov 6 total (calculated) [S1, S7].
- KOSPI had risen to around 9,000 earlier in the year [S1]. It closed at 5,059.69 on Nov 6. From Oct 30 to Nov 6 Samsung fell 16.13% and SK hynix fell 20.17% [S13].
- Kukmin Ilbo ties the selling to profit taking (selling to lock in gains) and portfolio rebalancing (selling to bring holdings back to target weights) as KOSPI rose from 4,000 to 9,000 [S1]. That is the paper's explanation. The flow data cannot test it.

## Heavy selling returned in November

- Foreigners net sold 18.68 trillion won of KOSPI stocks in October [S8]. The pace was uneven: about 1.87 trillion won a day from Oct 1 to 13 and about 0.43 trillion won a day from Oct 14 to 30 (calculated, Appendix A4).
- In the week of Nov 2 to 6 foreigners net sold 10.44 trillion won of KOSPI stocks [S12, S13]. Hankyung counted about 11.05 trillion won [S16]. The cause of the 0.6 trillion won gap was not found.
- Either weekly count is more than half of October's total, in five trading days (56% or 59%, calculated).
- The KOSPI daily average for Nov 2 to 5 was 1.94 trillion won, 108% above October's daily average [S8].
- That week foreigners net sold 5.36 trillion won of SK hynix and 2.18 trillion won of Samsung, 72% of the total [S12]. Hankyung's figures were 5.78 trillion and 2.57 trillion won [S16].
- The two stocks' share of new foreign selling has been near three quarters since September: 76.6% in September [S15], about 76% in October (calculated, Appendix A5) and 72% to 76% in the first week of November [S12, S16]. It was 89.1% from Jan 2 to May 20 [S17]. Selling has spread to other stocks since September. Sisajournal-e reports the same for the first week of November [S12].

## Foreign ownership

- Samsung was 45.95% foreign owned on Nov 6. Hankyung calls this the lowest since July 7, 2009 [S16], and Seoul Economic Daily also calls it a 17-year low [S13]. Kukmin calls an early October reading of about 46.4% the lowest since January 2008, when the ratio was 46.35% [S1]. Korea JoongAng Daily, also citing the Korea Exchange, dates that same 46.35% to Jul 16, 2009 [S34], which fits Hankyung's July 2009 comparison. Herald Business calls an Oct 19 reading similar to December 1998 [S5]. So the historical comparison is not settled, and Kukmin's 2008 date looks like the outlier.
- Herald Business puts Samsung's ownership at 52.4% earlier in the year, without a date or named source [S5].
- SK hynix was 48.27% foreign owned on Nov 6, the lowest since Nov 22, 2021 [S16]. Its 2026 peak was 54.64% on Feb 25 [S5, S34]. Korea JoongAng Daily gives the same figure and cites the Korea Exchange [S34].
- SK hynix's share count rose from 712,702,365 to 730,492,365 in July, when it issued 17,790,000 new shares to its ADR depositary for a Nasdaq listing [S39]. ADRs (American depositary receipts) are certificates traded in New York that stand for Korean shares. So its Feb 25 peak and Nov 6 ownership ratios are on different share counts. How the exchange counts depositary-held shares in the foreign ratio was not checked.
- SK hynix scheduled the cancellation of 23.69 million of its own shares for Nov 5 [S23]. Cancelling shares shrinks the share count, which raises the ratio with no trading. On the new count, 48.27% becomes about 49.9%, an increase of about 1.6 percentage points (calculated, Appendix A7). Whether the Nov 6 ratio already reflects the cancellation is not known.

## Who took the other side

Exchange data shows net trading by investor type, not by named buyer.

Korean papers had already described company buybacks as the main buyer against foreign selling, and warned of a gap in demand as the programs ended [S10, S18, S31, S32]. The comparisons by stock and by window in the next section are this note's own.

- In the first half, individuals were the main net buyers of the two stocks. They net bought 45.6 trillion won of Samsung and 40.8 trillion won of SK hynix [S1].
- In the second half other corporations, the investor type where company buybacks are recorded, were large net buyers of the two stocks, by Kukmin's count [S1]. Appendix C item 12 explains why Kukmin's Samsung figure is not relied on. In September other corporations net bought 31.40 trillion won of KOSPI stocks while foreigners net sold 21.51 trillion won [S15].

## What the company filings show

Samsung bought 28.25 trillion won of its own common shares in 2026 in four programs:

| Program window | Amount (won) | Shares | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan 8 to Jan 23 | 2,500,199,934,000 | 17,297,034 | Result report [S35]; half-year report [S19] |
| Jan 30 to Mar 10 | 3,572,799,991,850 | 19,496,637 | Result report [S40]; half-year report [S19] |
| Mar 23 to Apr 20 | 7,174,299,854,900 | 36,671,401 | Result report [S41]; half-year report [S19]; results announcement [S21] |
| Aug 24 to Oct 6 | 14,999,999,899,750 | 56,856,305 | Result report [S42]; fnnews report of it [S20] |

The last program paid an average of 263,823 won a share [S20, S42]. Samsung approved it on Aug 21 for employee compensation [S22, S43], so those shares are meant to go to staff, not to be cancelled. Samsung's Jan 29 acquisition decision gives employee share compensation as the purpose of the Jan 30 program [S43], and its Oct 29 disposal decision shows 73,359,314 treasury shares cancelled from the start of 2026 to Oct 29 [S45].

SK hynix bought 23,693,272 shares for 40.00 trillion won from Aug 20 to Oct 21, an average of 1,688,426 won a share [S24, S36]. The shares were to be cancelled on Nov 5 [S23, S24, S37]. Its board approved a second program of about 20 trillion won on Oct 28 [S26, S38]. It runs to Jan 28, 2027 [S27, S38]. About half is for shareholder returns and about half for employee compensation [S26, S38]. One report puts the second program at 1.25 million shares for 1.79 trillion won through Nov 4, buying about 250,000 shares a day [S28]. That is a single press source.

Together the two companies bought about 70 trillion won of their own shares in 2026 through Nov 4. That is about 38% of the 183.48 trillion won foreigners net sold in the two stocks (calculated, Appendix A6). This compares sizes only. Buyback orders and foreign sell orders are not matched trade for trade.

From Aug 20 to Oct 2 the two companies bought 47.68 trillion won. That was about 2.1 times foreign net selling of the two stocks in the same window [S10, S36, S42; calculated, Appendix A9].

Samsung's board approved on Aug 21 a 2026 shareholder return plan it expects to total about 90 to 110 trillion won [S22]. The 15 trillion won buyback for employee compensation appears in the same release as a separate item. On Oct 29 Samsung set a third quarter cash dividend of 4,601 won a share, about 30 trillion won in total [S25, S44]. Newsis reported that 35 trillion won of the plan was already paid or set, leaving 55 to 75 trillion won to be decided at the late January 2027 board, including substantial buybacks and cancellations [S25].

The filings show company buying in bursts:

- No buying by either company was found from Apr 21 to Aug 19. Foreign net selling from May to July was 103.23 trillion won [S2]. SK hynix's early 2026 cancellation used shares it already held [S29, S30].
- Samsung finished buying on Oct 6 and SK hynix's first program finished on Oct 21, before the November selling.
- SK hynix's second program bought 250,000 shares on Oct 29 [S27]. That is under half the 600,000 to 650,000 shares a day its first program bought through Oct 1, and below the 400,000 to 500,000 a day of that program's final three weeks [S18, S36].
- The program had up to about 18.2 trillion won, or 13.67 million shares, left in its plan after Nov 4 (calculated, Appendix A6). If SK hynix keeps the plan as filed, its share limit would bind first at recent prices: 13.67 million shares at the Nov 6 price of 1,132,000 won is about 15.5 trillion won [S13]. At 250,000 shares a day, buying would run at about 0.28 to 0.36 trillion won a day, depending on price [S13, S28]. That is below the 0.43 trillion won a day of foreign selling from Oct 14 to 30, and far below the 2.09 trillion won a day of Nov 2 to 6.
- A plan sets a ceiling on buying. Samsung's Jan 30 to Mar 10 program bought 19,496,637 of the 22,000,000 shares approved [S40, S43].

## What would change this view

- Foreign holdings counted in shares, rather than ratios, flattening for several weeks would suggest the selling is ending.
- The two stocks' share staying well below 70% over several weeks of heavy selling, with selling in KOSDAQ and Korean ETFs too, would suggest a broader exit from Korea.
- Single weeks are noisy. From Oct 26 to 30 foreigners net sold only 0.28 trillion won, and the two stocks were 5% of it [S11]. That article does not say whether its figure is KOSPI only.

## Appendix A: Method and calculations

All inputs come from the sources in Appendix B. Raw figures and their dates come before each calculation. Figures are in trillion won unless marked.

A1. Two-stock share, by date.

- Through Oct 8 [S1]: SK hynix 86.1399 + Samsung 85.5017 = 171.6416. Total 197.0861. Share 171.6416 / 197.0861 = 87.09%.
- Through Oct 12 [S2]: 87.1440 + 86.0570 = 173.2010. Total 199.1045. Share 86.99%.
- Through Oct 13 [S3]: about 176 / 203.6977 = 86.4%. The paper rounds to "about 86%". The 176 is itself rounded.
- Through Nov 4 [S6]: 94.4420 + 89.0370 = 183.4790. Total 213.6656 (A2). Share 85.87%.
- Through Nov 6: 188.0662 (A3) / 219.7590 [S7] = 85.58%.

A2. Rebuilding the total through Nov 4, and the cut-off question. No source gives the Nov 4 total directly.

- Through Oct 12: 199.1045 [S2].
- October: 18.6756 [S8]. Oct 1 to 12: 8.4635 [S2].
- Through Oct 30: 199.1045 + 18.6756 - 8.4635 = 209.3166.
- Cross-check: 219.7590 [S7] - 10.4394 (Nov 2 to 6) [S12] = 209.3196. The two agree within 0.003.
- Nov 2 to 4 (three trading days): 4.3490 [S6].
- Through Nov 4: 209.3166 + 4.3490 = 213.6656.
- Through Nov 5: 209.3166 + 7.7741 (Nov 2 to 5) [S8] = 217.0907.

Which date Jaekyung's stock figures end on:

- For Nov 4: the article describes November so far as "3 trading days", which is Nov 2 to 4 [S6].
- For Nov 5: it attributes the stock figures to "the Korea Exchange on the 5th" and was published after the close on Nov 5 [S6]. Korean papers often use that wording for data through the previous day, so this is not decisive.
- A test: eToday gives two-stock selling of 19.8655 from Oct 1 to Nov 5 [S8]. Jaekyung's figures minus Kukmin's Oct 8 figures give (94.4420 - 86.1399) + (89.0370 - 85.5017) = 11.8374 [S1, S6]. The two stocks' Nov 5 daily selling was 1.5121 + 1.1797 = 2.6918 [S9]. All foreign net selling from Oct 1 to 8 was 197.0861 - 190.6401 = 6.446 [S1, S2].
- On the Nov 4 reading, two-stock selling from Oct 1 to 8 = 19.8655 - 11.8374 - 2.6918 = 5.34, or 83% of 6.446.
- On the Nov 5 reading, it = 19.8655 - 11.8374 = 8.03, or 125% of 6.446.
- A two-stock share above 100% can happen when foreigners net buy other stocks. 125% would be unusual. But Oct 1 to 8 is only five trading days, and single weeks here range from 5% to 76%. So the test leans toward Nov 4 but is weak. Nov 4 is the more likely end date. It is not proven.
- On the Nov 5 reading: 183.4790 / 217.0907 = 84.52% through Nov 5, and (183.4790 + 1.8954) / 219.7590 = 84.35% through Nov 6. The 1.8954 is the Nov 6 daily figures for the two stocks [S9].

A3. Estimating the two stocks through Nov 6 (Nov 4 reading).

- Daily foreign net selling [S9]: SK hynix Nov 5 1.5121 and Nov 6 1.8054. Samsung Nov 5 1.1797 and Nov 6 0.0900.
- 183.4790 + 4.5872 = 188.0662.
- By stock: SK hynix 94.4420 + 3.3175 = 97.76. Samsung 89.0370 + 1.2697 = 90.31.
- This is an estimate. eToday's SK hynix daily figures for Nov 2 to 6 sum to 5.3148, against its own weekly figure of 5.3440 and Sisajournal-e's 5.3627 [S9, S12]. Using the weekly figures instead moves the 85.6% by less than 0.1 percentage point.

A4. Daily pace.

- Jan to Sep from Herald's monthly figures [S2]: 190.6401. Herald's September figure is 21.5246. Seoul Economic Daily gives 21.5108 [S15], a gap of 0.0138.
- Oct 1 to 13 (7 trading days): (203.6977 - 190.6401) / 7 = 1.865.
- Oct 14 to 30 (13 trading days): (18.6756 - 13.0576) / 13 = 0.432.
- October (20 trading days): 0.9338, and Nov 2 to 5: 1.9435 [S8].
- Nov 2 to 6: 10.4394 / 5 = 2.088 [S12].

A5. October two-stock figure.

- eToday gives Oct 1 to Nov 5: SK hynix 13.9222, Samsung 5.9433 [S8].
- SK hynix November days [S9]: 0.2323 + 0.6358 + 1.1292 + 1.5121 = 3.5094. October: 13.9222 - 3.5094 = 10.4128.
- Samsung November days: eToday gives Nov 3 to 6 only [S9]. Its weekly Samsung figure of 2.1667 implies Nov 2 was a net buy of 0.1319. So Nov 2 to 5 = -0.1319 + 0.2087 + 0.8202 + 1.1797 = 2.0767. October: 5.9433 - 2.0767 = 3.8666.
- October two stocks: 14.2794, which is 76.5% of 18.6756.

A6. The 38% buyback ratio, and what is left of SK hynix's second program.

- Samsung, four programs [S35, S40, S41, S42]: 2,500,199,934,000 + 3,572,799,991,850 + 7,174,299,854,900 + 14,999,999,899,750 = 28,247,299,680,500 won, for 130,321,377 shares.
- SK hynix, first program [S36]: 40,004,339,063,500 won for 23,693,272 shares. Edaily printed 40,004,339,060,000 [S24].
- SK hynix, second program through Nov 4 [S28]: 1.7904.
- Total: 28.2473 + 40.0043 + 1.7904 = 70.04.
- Foreign net selling of the two stocks through Nov 4 [S6]: 183.4790.
- Ratio: 70.04 / 183.4790 = 38.2%.
- By stock: Samsung 28.25 / 89.04 = 31.7%. SK hynix 41.79 / 94.44 = 44.3%.
- SK hynix second program left after Nov 4: planned 20.0077 [S28, S38] - 1.7904 = 18.2173. Shares: 14,920,000 planned [S27, S38] - 1,250,000 bought [S28] = 13,670,000. At the Nov 6 price of 1,132,000 won [S13]: 15.47.
- At 250,000 shares a day: 0.358 a day at the program's average price so far of 1,432,295 won [S28], or 0.283 a day at the Nov 6 price.

A7. SK hynix ownership after the Nov 5 cancellation.

- Shares before: 730,492,365. Shares after: 706,799,093 [S23]. Edaily prints 706,789,093 [S24]. 730,492,365 - 23,693,272 = 706,799,093, so Newspim's figure is used.
- 48.27% x 730,492,365 / 706,799,093 = 49.89%.

A8. Samsung's January program share count.

- The Jan 27 result report gives 17,297,034 shares at an average of 144,545 won [S35].
- Cross-check: the half-year report gives 73,465,072 common shares acquired from January to June [S19]. The second and third programs bought 19,496,637 and 36,671,401 [S40, S41]. 73,465,072 - 19,496,637 - 36,671,401 = 17,297,034, the same figure.

A9. Aug 20 to Oct 2 window [S10].

- Company buying, from the result reports' daily trades: Samsung 14.7078 [S42] + SK hynix 32.9722 [S36] = 47.6800. Samsung's figure is its 14,999,999,899,750 won total less the Oct 6 trade of 292,225,972,750 won. SK hynix's is its trades from Aug 20 to Oct 2. eToday printed 14.7077 and 32.9721 [S10], cut off rather than rounded.
- Foreign net selling of the two stocks [S10]: SK hynix 16.3134 + Samsung 6.1315 = 22.4449.
- Ratio: 47.6800 / 22.4449 = 2.12 times.
